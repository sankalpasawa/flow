### 🗂️ [2026-04-04] Deploy ₹5L in current market dip — beat 99%

**Refined idea:** Invest ₹5,00,000 during current market dip. Research-driven, not gut-feel. Goal: outperform 99% of market participants.

---

#### Claude's thinking

**The honest bar:** Beating 99% means outperforming nearly all retail investors AND most fund managers. Sustainable only with a genuine edge — not just picking good stocks, but having better information, better analysis, or better discipline than the crowd.

**Your real edges as an individual:**
1. **No benchmark pressure** — funds must be fully invested, you can wait for the perfect pitch
2. **Small size** — ₹5L can go into small/mid caps that funds can't touch (too illiquid for them)
3. **Long time horizon** — you don't have quarterly redemptions forcing you to sell
4. **Concentrated bets** — funds hold 40-80 stocks; you can hold 5-8 high-conviction picks

**Framework to beat the market with ₹5L:**

**Step 1 — Macro context (current dip)**
- Identify *why* market is down: global (US rates, geopolitics), sector-specific, or India-specific
- Dips driven by fear/sentiment = opportunity. Dips driven by earnings collapse = trap
- Rule: only buy companies where the dip is NOT caused by fundamental deterioration

**Step 2 — Stock screening criteria (eliminate 95% of stocks)**
- Market cap: ₹500Cr–₹10,000Cr (small/mid — less analyst coverage = mispricing opportunity)
- Revenue growth: >15% YoY for last 3 years
- ROCE: >15% consistently
- Debt/Equity: <0.5 (no leverage bombs)
- Promoter holding: >50%, not pledged
- Current price: >30% below 52-week high (dip filter)

**Step 3 — Deep research (the 99% separator)**
For each shortlisted stock:
- Read last 4 quarters of concalls (management tone, guidance vs actuals)
- Check credit rating reports and annual report footnotes (most people skip these)
- Map competitive moat: why can't a competitor replicate this in 3 years?
- Reverse DCF: what growth rate is the current price implying? Is that beatable?

**Step 4 — Portfolio construction**
- Max 6-8 stocks. Concentration = returns, diversification = mediocrity
- Position sizing: 20% max per stock, 15% in speculative/high-risk
- Keep 15-20% cash reserve for averaging down if thesis holds

**Step 5 — Pre-mortem before buying**
For each pick, write one paragraph: "It's 18 months later and this stock is down 50%. What happened?"
If you can't imagine a credible failure mode, you haven't researched enough.

**Step 6 — Exit discipline**
- Exit if thesis breaks (not because price fell)
- Take partial profits at 50% gain, let rest run
- Hard stop only if fundamentals deteriorate, NOT on price drops
